# How to Read EPUB Files on iPhone (Without iTunes or Apple Books)

**The short answer:** an iPhone cannot open an EPUB on its own. iOS has no system-level EPUB viewer outside Apple's own Books app, which is why tapping an EPUB in an email or a download often does nothing useful. Install a reader app, put the file on the phone, and you are reading — no cable, no iTunes, no account.

This guide covers the three ways to get an EPUB onto an iPhone, what it costs, and what to do when a file refuses to open.

## Three ways to get an EPUB onto your iPhone

All three work with [BiblioFuse Reader](/en/comicreader/), which is free on the App Store and renders EPUB files itself rather than handing them off to another app. Pick whichever suits where the file already is.

### 1. From the Files app

If the EPUB is already on the phone — downloaded in a browser, saved from a message, sitting in iCloud Drive — open **Files**, then copy it into the BiblioFuse folder. It appears in your library straight away.

The folder is visible in Files, so this works in both directions: you can drag books in from iCloud Drive on any device, and you can get them back out again later. Nothing is hidden inside an app container you cannot reach.

### 2. By AirDrop

AirDrop the EPUB from a Mac, iPad or another iPhone, and choose **Copy to BiblioFuse** when iOS asks what to open it with. EPUB is registered as a document type the app handles, so it appears in that list with no setup.

One nuance worth knowing: `.cbr` and `.rar` files are not registered as AirDrop document types, so "Copy to BiblioFuse" may not appear for those two. Copying them into the visible Files folder works normally. EPUB, PDF, CBZ, ZIP and TXT all AirDrop fine.

### 3. Over Wi-Fi, from any computer

Turn on **Wi-Fi Transfer** in the app's settings. The phone starts a small transfer page on your local network and shows you the address; open that address in a browser on your Mac or PC and drop files onto it.

Nothing travels over the internet, no cable is involved, and it handles a whole folder of books in one go. This is the fastest route if you are moving a collection rather than a single file.

## You do not need iTunes, Finder syncing, or an account

None of the three methods above involves iTunes or Finder device sync — the cable-and-sync flow that used to be the only way to get a book onto an iPhone, and that could overwrite what was already on the device.

- **No cable**, and no sync step that replaces your library
- **No account and no sign-in.** You are not asked to register to read a file you already own
- **No upload.** The file is read where you put it; nothing is sent to a server for processing

## Reading it once it is there

EPUB files come in two shapes, and both are handled.

**Reflowable** books — novels, most non-fiction — let you set the font, size and layout, and the text re-wraps to fit whatever you choose. **Fixed-layout** EPUBs, which is how many comics and illustrated books are packaged, keep their original page design and are read page by page.

- Vertical Japanese text (tategaki), and right-to-left page order for comic-style EPUBs
- Text-to-speech for EPUB and TXT, using the voices already installed on your device
- Search inside a book, plus bookmarks, ratings and tags
- Your place is kept per book, and follows you between devices if you use the iCloud library

## When an EPUB will not open

Almost always one of two reasons.

**The file is DRM-protected.** Books bought from most large stores are encrypted and locked to that store's own app. No third-party reader can open them — not because it lacks the feature, but because the file is not readable without the store's keys. Check whether the shop you bought from offers a plain download; many indie publishers, Humble Bundle, and Project Gutenberg do.

**The file is not really an EPUB.** Occasionally a download arrives with an `.epub` name but different contents. Opening it on a computer will usually reveal what it actually is.

This is where a DRM-free collection is genuinely easier to live with: files you own outright can be read in whatever app you prefer, moved between devices, and backed up normally. Nothing expires and nothing is tied to one company staying in business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an iPhone open EPUB files without any app?

Not really. iOS has no general EPUB viewer of its own — Apple Books is an app like any other, and a file not added to it has nowhere to open. Any reader app fills that gap; the difference between them is what else they do with the file once it is open.

### Is there a free EPUB reader for iPhone?

Yes. BiblioFuse Reader is free to download, and reading EPUBs stored on the device is free with no page or book limit. The paid tier covers cloud sync, streaming from your own computer or server, and live translation.

### Do I have to use iTunes to put books on my iPhone?

No. iTunes and Finder device sync are not involved in any of the three methods above. Wi-Fi Transfer in particular needs nothing but a browser on the same network.

### Can I read the same book on my iPad and Mac too?

Yes. BiblioFuse is a single universal purchase across iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision Pro, and if you keep your library in your own iCloud storage the book and your place in it appear on all of them. That cloud library is part of the paid tier.

### What else does it open besides EPUB?

PDF, CBZ, CBR, RAR, ZIP and TXT, in the same library as your EPUB files — so a collection of novels and a collection of comics do not need two apps. See the [full feature list by platform](/en/features/).
